Some important memory/locality properties already match the optimized source, so they are poor targets for speculative rewrites:

- The nucleotide scan borrows packed subject storage directly ([nucleotide_setup.rs:335](../src/nucleotide_setup.rs#L335)). It does not expand every genome for scanning. The in-memory database deliberately retains approximately `N + ceil(N/4)` residue bytes (expanded NCBI4na plus packed NCBI2na), plus headers, ambiguity data and short readable scanner tails. This costs RAM in exchange for cheap exact ambiguity-aware range access; it is not evidence of waste by itself. Original SeqDB instead exposes packed sequence storage and retrieves/restores data through its sequence-source layer. Changing this tradeoff needs RSS/bandwidth evidence, not just lower byte counts.
- Hot protein lookup cells preserve source-sized packed layouts: [AaLookupSmallboneCell](../src/core/blast_aalookup.rs#L27) is explicitly 12 bytes/alignment4; the ordinary cell preserves its source payload. With word3/28-letter encoding the small backbone has 28,540 cells, or 342,480 cell bytes before its presence vector/overflow. Source uses the same table geometry. The transient thin backbone is freed after finalization ([blast_aalookup.rs:173](../src/core/blast_aalookup.rs#L173)); it is not retained as a second scan table. `DiagStruct` retains a single packed u32 ([blast_extend.rs:54](../src/core/blast_extend.rs#L54)), and source diagonal offset/aging is preserved.
- HSP arrays are contiguous pointer/owner slots in both versions; original HSPs are separately allocated too. `Box`/`Vec` alone does not prove worse layout. Likewise, original matrices have separately allocated rows. Do not flatten/reorder HSP heaps or change lookup representation without preserving tie/order behavior and measuring the actual hot access pattern.
- Larger query batches increase lookup neighborhood counts, overflow chains, diagonal working sets and the number of query/subject lists in **both** programs. The current small real batches contain eight queries. Borrowing/reusing buffers can reduce adapter cost; changing query batching or subject order can change diagonal history and bounded-selection ties, so it is a separate algorithm-sensitive experiment.
